# Resume & portfolio class: the gist

Session on building a product manager resume and portfolio. The speaker had reviewed 500–700 resumes.

## Resume

| Topic | Advice |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Clear a basic bar (about 7.5–8 out of 10). After that, your own story makes the difference. |
| **Length** | 1 page. 2 pages only with 10+ years of experience, and with no empty space. Use A4. |
| **Summary** | Optional for freshers. Otherwise 2–4 lines: years of experience, industry or domain, main skills. Don't split "2 years PM + 5 years IT"; say "7 years of experience". |
| **Words to avoid** | "Aspiring", "motivated", "committed to", "I", "me", "my". Don't stuff keywords so it reads like ChatGPT. |
| **Every bullet** | What you did → how (tools, methods, frameworks) → the impact (numbers, internal or external). 1–2 lines max, never 3. |
| **Bad bullet** | Job-description lines like "Created product vision and strategy" or "Ran sprint planning". Say *which* strategy, *what* you shipped, *what changed*. |
| **Bullets per job** | Newest job 5–6, the one before 4, older ones 3. Jobs 10+ years ago: 1–2 lines. Don't skip jobs (gaps show), just shorten them. |
| **Order** | Name and contact (clickable LinkedIn, email, portfolio, any certification worth noticing) → summary → experience → education → certifications → tools and skills. Recent graduates put education on top. |
| **Company vs title first** | A well-known company (Microsoft, CRED, Razorpay)? Put the company first. A strong title at a lesser-known company? Put the title first. |
| **Leave out** | Hobbies, tables, colours, logos, photos, graphs, age, languages (unless applying abroad), a separate "client projects" section. |
| **ATS** | No single "ATS-friendly" format exists: Workday, Lever and Greenhouse all differ. Keep a clean structure and good content. |
| **Tools** | Tools are enablers. Learn the basics of Jira or Figma in 2–3 hours, then list them. |
| **Certifications** | Only ones that add credibility for the role (CSPO, PMP, SAFe). Not every online course. |
| **Tailoring** | Fine to tweak for each job. Keep 1–2 base versions and match each job post's overlap. Apply where your domain or industry overlaps. |
| **Career switchers** | Show how closely you worked with product: discovery, research, PRDs, launches. Target roles that overlap your domain or tech. |
| **Cover letters** | Mostly not needed now. Portfolios carry more weight. |

## Portfolio

| Topic | Advice |
|---|---|
| **Why** | Proof of work: product sense, thinking, data, clarity, storytelling. |
| **Where to build** | Notion first (or WordPress or Webflow). Then mirror it on UXfolio, Behance or Dribbble so it shows up in Google. Make slides in Google Slides (easy to share). |
| **What to add (best first)** | Your own side project (idea → research → prototype → small launch) → improvement case studies ("improve Groww retention") → product teardowns → articles or PRDs. |
| **How many** | At least 3 strong ones. A weak portfolio can cost an offer, so get feedback first. |
| **Where to show** | LinkedIn Featured section, LinkedIn posts, a link on your resume, emails to recruiters. |

- **Education goes first** for recent graduates (finished this year or last year, still studying, or no jobs yet).
- The AI is told to keep bullets to 2 lines, keep the same number of bullets, and avoid first person and filler words. The cleaner removes "aspiring", "motivated" and "passionate" if the AI adds them.
